Why Rural Broadband Matters

Millions of rural households still lack internet that is fast enough for remote work, online school, and telehealth. Long distances, low population density, and rough terrain make it expensive to run cable to every home. The good news is that several technologies can close that gap, and choosing the right one starts with understanding how each works.

Three Ways Internet Reaches Rural Homes

Fiber

Glass strands carry light from the provider to your home. Fiber offers the highest speeds and lowest latency, but it is costly to build over long rural distances.

Fixed Wireless

A radio link connects an antenna on your home to a nearby tower. CBRS fixed wireless can deliver fiber-like speeds within a few miles, often with no trenching required.

Satellite

A dish talks to satellites in orbit, so service is available almost anywhere. Low-Earth-orbit systems have greatly reduced latency compared with older satellites.
